What limits the comparison

  • Same inputs: every bot decides from v1's logged rows, so games, lines, quotes and timing are identical. A cutoff v1 misses (computer asleep) is missed by every model.
  • Bot 1 only has money at risk when a resting order fills, so it has fewer and smaller positions; its fills come from public trade prints, and our pretend order never affected other traders.
  • The bots skip lines with a one-sided book (they need a mid-price); v1 can still buy a side that has sellers. In the 2025-26 data this changed almost nothing.
  • Daily caps: every model except v1 no daily limit counts its $100 cap by the UTC date of tip-off, as v1 always has, but only over its own trades. On Oct 24 v1's early games shared a cap with its Oct 23 late games (same UTC date) while the bots started empty, so v1 skipped some trades the bots could take that day. v1 no daily limit has no cap at all; that is its only difference from v1.
  • Bot 2's weights and Bot 3's selection both used the 2025-26 season, so their backtests are not clean held-out tests (see each tab). Only the forward test counts as evidence.
  • Probability scores use two-sided lines only. Correlated lines (a ladder of strikes for one team) make differences look more certain than they are.
  • v1 at 2 hours decides an hour earlier from its own rows, so its prices and injury news differ from everyone else's on the same games; that difference is what it tests.
  • The in-game bots buy during games, at moments and prices the pre-game models never see, and only while the in-game monitor is running (it starts with the scheduler shortly before tip-off). They began on Oct 25, 2026, so their history is shorter, and their CLV is measured about 2 minutes after each buy rather than at tip-off.
  • No winner is declared from short-run profit. Verdicts follow each bot's PREREGISTRATION.md, once, at the end of the 2026-27 regular season.

ArbitrageTrading

Looks for NBA arbitrage: opposite bets on the same game, market and line at two platforms whose prices, after fees, lock in a profit whatever the result. OddsPapi's free price history for each game is read after tip-off, and the frozen rules are replayed on it from six hours before tip: a bot looking every 60 seconds and placing legs 30-45 seconds later (By hand, the primary profile), up to $1,000 per opportunity. A leg fills only if its price is still there when it arrives; a failed leg is hedged. This is a prospective-rule replay, so it cannot show that orders would have been seen in time or accepted.
